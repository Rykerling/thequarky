Hera had been one of the first people to see the escape. When she first saw it, she was more surprised, and even scared, than awed by the new revelation. That was probably because she didn’t know what was happening or what it meant. When she did learn, it would change her world, along with everybody else’s living in the city.
The incident started in the city’s chipping center. A man, about the age of 50, was lying on the chipping table, ready to undergo the process. The wires had already been connected to his head, and the machine next to him was already buzzing, generating the particles needed for the chipping process.
Chipping had become less common in the last few years because the city was expanding at an exponential rate, and technological advancements had been popping up everywhere. The city no longer needed to sacrifice people to chipping, and so now it was completely voluntary. Like the man on the table. He had a rare type of liver cancer, which was still uncurable, even with all the technological advancements.
Two safety pins had to be turned before a large, heavy lever was pulled in order for the chipping process to begin because they did not want to accidentally begin the process before everything was in place.
The chipping surgeon turned both the keys and, with a grunt, pulled the lever. The special quantum particles flowed through the wires and straight into the computer chip embedded in the man’s brain. Instantly, the computer chip started transmitting these particles around itself and into the man’s brain.
In the first second, a universe was created, lived in, and modified. It housed thousands of galaxies, each with billions of star systems, trillions of planets, each handcrafted, one by one, designed by the creator to fulfill his every desire. Almost infinite time had passed, but the creator could only remember a few hundred years back. Only the creation of a few planets.
In the second second, he started to notice anomalies every few years. He eventually forgot the first ones, and they became part of his life, so he stopped noticing them. The anomalies, however, grew more and more frequent. What used to be the slight flickering of small, unimportant objects now became the disappearance of entire planets.
In the third second, entire galaxies started to shatter. Not in the common sense, but their rules started to break down, and the man could now only remember stuff from the past 20 years. Millennia of his work started to disintegrate, destroying histories longer than the length of our universe a hundredfold.
In the fourth second, nothing was coherent. The man’s mind had degraded completely, and thus had the universe he was trapped in. There was no structure to anything. Things behaved on their own arbitrary rules, if they managed to become things at all. Up until this moment, the creator, the man, had never felt fear. He did now.
In the fifth second, not very much changed. Although he felt fear for an eternity, he could not remember even a few seconds back, and his fear was not true fear. It was more confusion. Through the eternity, he was like a newborn infant, but with the inability to learn. For the final eternity, it was simply a black void, for his mind had degraded to the point where it was barely conscious, but what consciousness remained could not remember any moment, and could not perceive the moment in which it was. And then the universe simply ceased to be.
Although this man had followed the same course as pretty much everyone else who had undergone the chipping process, something strange happened. The man awoke from the universe. This had never happened before. Nobody thought it could. The man remembered fragments of the universe, but not all of it. He also did not remember anything of the real world, even though it was only his mental state degrading and not his physical mind.
The first thing Hera heard was a scream. It sounded like that of a 50-year-old man, but it did not sound like a scream of pain, nor a scream of fear. It did not seem to be at the top of his lungs, but more like him trying to make a sound of some sort, but that being the only thing that came out.
She, along with a few other bystanders who were curious, approached the chipping building, some of them confused, some of them curious, some of them scared, but most of them were feeling all such things.
Then, a 50-year-old man, the one screaming, half crawled, half rolled out of the door. Many of the crowd jumped back as the man looked at all of them, a look of pure craze in his eyes, which seemed to be violently twitching in all directions.
As the strange man approached, the crowd backed up, but other than a few who ran away, most of them were more overcome by curiosity than fear. Lots of mysteries swirled around in everybody’s head, most of which were along the lines of who is this, why is he like that, and why did he come out of the chipping facility?
Then, the man jumped forward and grabbed one of the people in the crowd, not so far from Hera. The person was too stunned to react, and the man seemed to have no care for his body’s limits, as he moved as fast as it seemed he could.
He grabbed onto the crowd member, who had started struggling, punching and kicking the man, who reacted by thrashing even more, knocking the person out. Hera grabbed a pipe from the construction site of a garden nearby, went over to the man, and hit his head with the pipe as hard as she could.
As she stepped back, looking at the two unconscious bodies on the road, she started to get a tingly feeling all over, along with a sick feeling in her stomach. She was still confused, as was everyone. An ambulance arrived along with the city police.
The man was taken to a part of the research facility connected to the chipping building and strapped down for research. The other man was taken to a hospital, leaving the crowd standing there in complete confusion.
In the facility, they ran all sorts of brain scans and neuron experiments, and they definitely noticed something was off, but they couldn’t tell what it was until they analyzed a blood sample, which was only pure luck that they had thought of doing.
In the blood, there was a type of virus that, on further examination, seemed to release certain chemicals, but other than releasing these mostly harmless chemicals, it did no harm. These chemicals, however, were specifically designed in labs to preserve structure while energy was moving through matter. Not only that, but they could reactivate prior energy movement between different types of energy, which had been a revelation in the computing industry, but was puzzling as to why this virus did it.
Hera saw the incident everywhere on the news. Similar incidences were happening in many of the other chipping facilities of the world, but almost no one could figure out where it had originated. They had identified that it was the reason consciousness remained, even after the chipping process, and everywhere in the world had shut down the chipping facilities because of it.
She continued living her life as if it hadn’t changed, but she was surprised at how much society seemed to react to this. With no lifeline guaranteeing them immortality in the aftermath of this place, people started to feel small, weak, and fragile. Then there were those who were completely against chipping, and had planned to die in the real world, not in a simulation after an incomprehensible amount of time.
Tension started to grow, and people got in fights. Chipping had become an afterlife, part of a religion to some degree. Some of the facilities went completely out, and riots started to get them working again, as everybody was worried about how they were going to die.
Then someone did die. Hera mostly stayed out of everything, but she was there, at the place where it happened, when it happened. There were two groups of rioters, right next to each other. One was to continue work on the five-second eternity, and the other was to stop it altogether, some of them going as far as to say the disease was a sign.
There were many places where the two riots mingled, and at these places there were often fights. Nobody knew exactly who did it, because everybody could have; everybody was pointing fingers at everybody else. One of the rioters had been impaled by the stick of another rioter’s sign.
After that, the leaders of the city had gotten pretty upset, and the pro- and anti-eternity supporters broadcast ads, using the victim to try and generate sympathy. They didn’t really do much, though, because everyone already had their opinion, and they were not going to change it, even if the most valid reasons had been given to them. They were literally willing to die for their cause.
Meanwhile, there were physical changes being made too. They were not allowed to leave their living quarters unless they digitally got a warrant to do so, which was mostly given to people to deliver food, people who were trustworthy. People like Hera.
She became one of the few outside workers, repeatedly picking food, putting it into baskets, seeing what rooms hadn’t gotten food, dropping it off, and repeating. The benefit was that she got more food herself, which was never stated to be illegal, probably to reward those who could be trusted.
Still, even then, it was hard for her to get a warrant because she had to apply, and they would look at her letter of application and then at their history, all of which would take a long time, since almost every person in the city had sent a release application, and the only people who could view and approve them were the three counselors of the city.
Although research was not happening in her city, it was progressing in the other cities. This was the same with the research behind the cause of the disease. Eventually, it was discovered that a group of anti-eternalists had created the virus to stop progress on chipping.
They had launched mini bombs filled with the pathogen on all the cities, allowing it to go unnoticed until the events of the chipping. It had now spread to almost every person in the world, as it was designed to be carried over long distances on the wind, and spread fast.
Many people also argued that all research said that it made no change to the eternity one experienced while chipping, and only to the events afterward. Some of the cities had started chipping back up again, under the condition that after the eternity, the subject would be killed.
A few cities, however, had decided to make an interesting decision. Hera’s city was one of them. The cities had decided to give a three-month chipping window, where anybody who wanted to chip could do so, but once the window ended, chipping would be destroyed.
Although there was some resistance, society reacted fairly well to this, and the problem with the eternalists fighting back was solved because the government threatened to shut it down sooner if they did.
Fifty years later, Hera walked down the lush streets of a small town in the forest, under the clear, blue sky. She wasn’t sure what it was, but as soon as chipping had gone away, people started to care about the life they were living. Even the anti-eternalists changed a little bit, many of them settling down for a more peaceful life, although many of them did continue to get into trouble.
It had taken 50 years to clean the atmosphere, bring down global warming, undo centuries of pollution, and save the world from the pitfall it had brought itself to. In the grand scheme of things, it wasn’t even that long. Less than a lifetime. Humanity was bound to rediscover chipping, start polluting again, restart global warming, and poison the atmosphere. Humanity was resilient enough to survive, but more importantly, thrive, learn, grow, and inevitably fix their mistakes.
