En slid his fingers over the rough rock as he slowly crept forward. He could hear frequencies in the distance.
He was making a mental map of the entire cave in his head based on the sound frequencies that were ricocheting off the walls of the cavern. These frequencies were special, so they would be unnoticeable from any distance other than very near.
He moved very slowly and only when his group of prey was not emitting frequencies. They would know sooner or later, though. He had to hurry, but he couldn't go too fast. In his world, a disturbed pebble was one of the loudest things. It would be a dead giveaway to his location.
Suddenly, the other group went silent. They knew they were being hunted. En knew they would be extra careful not to make a sound. He also knew they would be moving.
Anyone who knew they were being stalked and stayed in the same spot was easy prey. This was what he was waiting for. He sprawled out flat, letting all of his appendages go as far from his body as possible. From every part of his skin, he could feel the sharp pricks of the rocks against his scabbed body.
En had torn up all of his skin from the moment he could. Before that, his group would cut him up for him. The scabs on his skin resembled the rough surface of the rocks more than his smooth skin did. The one downside was that scabbing your skin released a potent smell that attracted everyone. When he was fully scabbed, his skin acted like scales, so he didn't need to scratch it anymore.
In some places, he could feel his scabbed skin locking into the crevices in the rock that surrounded him. He waited there for what felt like an eternity, but he knew that this had to be done if he was going to eat.
Finally, he felt a tentatively moving hand slowly lower onto his finger. As soon as they made contact, he jumped out and started stabbing with his long, sharp claw-like fingernails, hoping he would hit something vital and kill them before they could react.
They were able to react, though. En felt a stab near his foot and then another higher on his leg. His fingers were dripping blood now.
The noisy commotion was lighting up his mental map and giving him a good idea of how his prey was oriented. He stabbed at his opponent one more time. His hand plunged in, breaking through the rib cage, grabbing the heart, and twisting with all his might. He could hear the blood vessels ripping and his attacker go limp.
He could hear another commotion nearby as one of his group members fought against one of the people from the other group. En put the body on top of himself and walked silently away. He knew which path to take to avoid the others in his group. He didn't want to be mistaken for the prey. They had a certain cave about a mile away that they had set as a meeting point for the survivors of the hunt.
Later, the rest of his group arrived. In all, they had three kills. The killers always got to eat the hearts of their victims, since they were the ones who worked and used up the most nutritious resources found in the heart. The rest of the meat would be divided evenly among the rest of the group. They would eat it all very quickly and then store the meat in their bodies, slowly digesting it over the next month.
Contact with other groups was rare, so they had to be able to deal with the hunger during the vast voids between encounters. All the groups spoke the same language, but the people in the groups never talked very much. This was because talking often gave away their location.
En walked over to his sleeping nook. He curled up inside it and slept. None of his skin moved from his breathing. It was too crusted with scabs. From the outside, sound would bounce off it like an ordinary rock. For as long as he could remember, En had been repeating this routine.
Every time he hunted and killed, he always felt a small ping of something painful inside. He didn't know that all of his kind felt this when they killed. It was the only way of life they knew. It was the only emotion they ever felt. Every once in a while, En would think about whether there was any other possible way. As long and as hard as he thought about that, though, he realized there wasn't. If they stopped eating each other, they would die of starvation.
These were some of the thoughts he had before he fell asleep. He always fell asleep thinking these thoughts after a hunt, whether it had been successful or not.
The next wake time, En awoke with extra energy. He always did after a successful hunt. It was probably the new food adding energy to his body. This energy was what would drive him forward and away from his group over the next few days.
En had plenty of smart ideas, such as sprawling out instead of running in and attacking during hunts. One of his other smart ideas was that everybody would split up after hunting, and a little less than a month later they would all regroup there for another hunt. That way, if one of them got eaten, the rest would still be safe.
He spent the rest of the day moving through the tunnels. En remembered every tunnel he'd been in with perfect detail. That was how his species worked. They built a model with echolocation, and that model would stay in their heads forever.
The next few days were very uneventful. Sometimes on his journeys, En would come across groups, but he would never attack because he couldn't hold any more food. That's what the gatherings were for.
He would, however, need water. He could go a month without food, but he could not go a month without water. In fact, the longest his species could go without water was about two weeks. That was his main goal while wandering. Water sources were constantly appearing and drying up, so he always had to be on the move to find new water. In En's experience, water always made a trickling sound.
The main upside to this was that water sources were relatively easy to find, and once he was within a mile, he could pinpoint the exact location of the stream. The downside was that since the trickling was so enormously loud, if any other group got near, they would be able to build a perfect model in their heads. That meant that if En wasn't hiding when they entered the sound space of the stream, then he couldn't hide at all.
He would often go in as fast as he could, since his sounds were mostly drowned out by the small stream. Then he would drink as much as he could as fast as he could, and then move far away from the stream. He would do this until the stream dried up.
Currently, however, he hadn't drunk since hunting. His task now was to find water. He wandered the endless cavern system for the rest of the wake time. Then he settled down into a small nook in the cave wall and slept.
The next wake time, he continued to search. He was about halfway through his wake time when he heard the faintest trickling sound in the distance. He had found water at last. He had already located the position of the stream based on how the sound was bouncing off the walls.
For the next few days, he kept going in and out of the water's sound radius. Soon, though, like all streams, it dried up.
En continued to wander through the caves. A few days after leaving the water, he came across something very interesting. There was a larger amount of pebbles in this part of the cave. There were also small grains, like sand, and even smaller grains that he was not familiar with. This was very interesting to him. He deduced that they were coming from the ceiling.
He started climbing up the walls. One of the rocks crumbled beneath his feet, making a small noise that, to him, was ear-splintering, since he was so used to the extreme quiet.
He huddled against the wall, not daring to move for a while. Finally, he gathered his courage and continued upward. When he got to the ceiling, the rocks fell off easily, sending out more ear-splintering sounds. He was holding onto the few stable rocks on the ceiling.
He didn't know that this was a clay deposit, meaning it was weak. He did, however, know that this was a very high point in the cave system. This was, in fact, the highest point in the cave system he had ever explored.
He could hear small cracks forming throughout the rock above. Something very strange was happening. Since the cracks were coming from inside the rock, he could map out its interior. He could tell where the cracks were. They were constantly forming and growing.
En decided to move away from the crumbling area on the ceiling. He felt the ceiling start to give way and leaped onto the wall just as it collapsed behind him.
The sound itself was horrifying. It was one of the loudest sounds he had ever heard. It would be alerting his kind everywhere in the caves. He decided he had to move quickly.
Where the ceiling had crumbled, there was now a gaping hole. He climbed through it, knowing his kind would close in from all sides.
When he was above the hole, he had to let out a little screech just to get his bearings.
The land it revealed was horrifying. There was no ceiling or walls. As far as the sound could bounce, there were only wide, flat, open grains of rock. It was also considerably hotter up there.
He could feel some of the scabbed skin on his fingertips and toes start to sizzle. He didn't pay much attention to that, however. He was used to pain. Pain never affected him.
What he was paying attention to was a large group of towers in the distance. Enormous pillars, all clustered together, reaching toward what should have been a ceiling. From them, much sound was being produced.
The sound reaching him would've been inaudible to most humans and creatures. En, however, was able to pick it up and make an almost accurate model of them. There were thirty enormously tall towers, reaching hundreds of body lengths high. One stood out, almost twice the size of the others. Surrounding these towers were even smaller towers. Those towers were surrounded by even smaller towers, and so on and so on.
At the bottom of each tower, low rumbling sounds were being emitted. This was what produced most of the sound that En was picking up. The pillars were multiple miles away.
Closer, however, was a small group of oddly shaped columns only a few bodies high. They had strange, thin, flexible sheets everywhere. Their pillars were harder.
En could tell the density of an object when sound reverberated off it.
He didn't know that these were called trees, specifically palm trees.
There were also smaller groups of these large, flexible sheets, which he didn't know were called leaves. This was the only small group of them, which he didn't know was called an oasis.
The rest of the space was vast and open. Everything was so blurry because of the way sound bounced off the small particles of sand.
He progressed toward the oasis, which was a very unfamiliar shape to him. He could tell that in the center there was a small pool of water, but the strange thing about this water was that it was not moving.
It was also strange that, with the pillars emitting sound miles away, everything was still illuminated with sound. Then he noticed there was another living creature in the oasis. This one walked on two legs instead of four and was carrying a strange upside-down dome shape. It was filled with soft, crinkly things.
He then thought that his skin wouldn't blend in with the sand around him, and the smell of burning scabs would reach this strange creature.
The creature, however, was not able to smell such a weak scent. En continued to think. He did not know how to fight the strange creature, but it would not know how to fight him. The creature was also making much sound as it walked. Whatever this creature was, it was definitely not one of En's kind.
He deduced that the only way to survive was to attack it. If it was dead, it could not kill him. He leaped, then ran forward and tackled the strange creature. Surprisingly, its skin ripped just by contact with his sharp scabs. The creature emitted such a loud sound that it hurt En's ears. He stabbed his fingers in and killed it immediately. He tasted it.
It tasted a lot like his own.
A thought was formulating in his head. Maybe his kind didn't have to eat one another. Maybe they could live in harmony. Maybe these creatures were the key to every one of his kind's problems. They wouldn't have to live in fear of each other, but instead in companionship with one another. His idea had formed.
The next few days fell into place perfectly. He went to the hole. En screeched as loudly as he could, sending a message through the cave system. Then he waited until he could hear a group approaching.
He said, "Find better source of food. Up here, strange land. Weak creatures. Good food. Spread word. Five days. I want as many of us as we can get."
He threw them a piece of the creature before they got close to him, and they ate it. They agreed.
He repeated this process for the next three groups that came over the following three days. After that, the caves were filled. He threw small amounts of meat to everyone to prove it was real.
Gathered in the cave were hundreds, maybe even thousands, of his kind. All were ones who believed they could live a better life. He was not going to let them down.
Of course, there were even more of his kind who had not arrived. They believed it was a trick. He sent them onward toward the pillars. He knew there were many of the weak creatures there, and they could feed easily.
The cave systems were as dense as sponges and stretched farther than he thought the desert did. For once, his kind could talk freely without being scared for their lives.
On the surface, he could hear the shapes of his kind running off into the distance.
Running toward the pillars that were a city—City 62—but he didn't know it was called that.
They were running off into the vast expanse.
