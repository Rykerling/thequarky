Zan sped across the lunar surface in his transpod, going in a direct line towards the Arc Rift. The Arc Rift had opened eight years ago, back when Zan was still ten. He couldn't remember anything from before the awakening, when God revealed his power through the rift. When humanity's purpose became clear.
He was grateful that his purpose had become clear, because it made the miserable life in which he was forced to live worth it. He could not remember ever being happy. Only being cold and hungry. To be one of the chosen for the rift meant it was hard to get food and good housing. Only through choosing the implant could he be able to have a better life.
There was another lunar city directly in the way, which was of annoyance to him, but his transpod was running low on battery and oxygen. It did unfortunately mean he had to interact with the atheists.
Atheists, they were known as, were people who did not believe in the great purpose. People who disrespected what God had put down, or didn't believe in it altogether. They despised the people of the great purpose, trying to disprove God with their science. They said that it was all a quantum anomaly from new types of quantum particles interacting with the brain.
Zan was not going to fall into the despair of atheism though. It sounded horrible to be bound by such a way of life, with their special radiation-blocking technology embedded into their brains, preventing them from knowing the will of God. The two groups despised each other.
The town was growing in the distance, and much farther the green glow of the Arc Rift. A small red LED blinked, indicating that the power was low, and he set course to a nearby charging and transpod trade station, where he could get a new, faster transpod.
In 10 minutes, he had made it to one of the ports, gotten out, and begun searching for a car trade station. It did not take him long to find one. The small bell rang quietly as he entered, the light tinkling sound alerting the shop manager of his presence. He had a flicker of déjà vu, but it was gone soon enough.
He got up from whatever he was working on to come greet Zan, squinting at him slightly as he came over, as if there was something odd about him that he couldn't quite place.
"Welcome to Han's auto shop, are you looking to borrow a transpod from the system, or do you need repairs?"
"I'm looking to borrow from the system." The way the repair shop manager, probably Han, was looking at him made him uncomfortable.
"Great, I have five options for you, the cheapest only five quark."
He had not been expecting to find such a good deal, although it seemed Han was a bit dim, as he had not figured Zan was a man of the purpose. Hopefully he wouldn't notice until he had a car.
"Right this way." Han motioned towards a hallway from the back of the shop.
They began walking down the hallway into a new larger room, with five transpods positioned and ready to drive out onto the lunar surface as soon as the garage door opened.
"There's something odd about you, I can't quite seem to place it. Maybe you look familiar? Have you ever been here before?"
Han considered this, continuing to look at him, before he was struck by realization. "You're one of the cultists. You're missing the implant scab on your forehead. You disgrace, get out of my shop now!"
"No, please just let me get a transpod, I'll take the cheapest one for 100 quark! You could buy a better transpod for that, with enough extra that you could quit your business altogether!"
"Get out of my shop!" Han had grabbed a thin but long metal pipe, approaching Zan in a menacing fashion.
He was not going to let this stop him from getting where he wanted to go. He grabbed a large metal bolt from a table next to him, a bit bigger than his fist, gripping it hard. The small carvings ground into his hand, just shy of breaking skin. The atmosphere was cold, as it always was on the moon, and much of this frigidity had absorbed into the bolt.
Han took one more menacing step forward, and Zan chucked the bolt. It launched through the air, as he had put as much force as he could muster into the singular action. He could hear the sound of the air moving out of the way.
There was a wet crack as Han's skull cracked on the impact, his stunned face frozen in place for a moment before it fell limp along with his body. The pipe he was holding clattered to the ground a moment or two before Han followed. A large pool of blood formed around his head, seeping out of a large dent where the skull had simply broken in, suspended out of the mushy brain at an awkward angle.
Nobody was going to get in the way of the great purpose.
The skulls of those who had grown up on the lunar surface were weaker, along with every other bone in the body, because of the low gravity. It would not have fractured so easily otherwise.
Zan was out of the shop and driving across the lunar surface within minutes. There would be no more interruptions, no more stops. His purpose was about to be fulfilled. All the misery in his life that he had suffered was finally paying off.
Some of the instruments in his transpod had stopped working, probably because of the divine force emanating from the rift. The engine was still working fine though, and he was getting close enough to the Arc Rift to see the skeletal remnants of all sorts of different types of transportation, crumpled up so their walls protruded in jagged angles.
There were also bodies. The bodies close to the bottom still had skin, although their bones had been broken and bent in weird angles, and there were smoking holes where their eyes should have been, along with a stream of blood floating up from their mouth, sometimes splattering on the floating hunks of metal above them, as if gravity had simply ceased to exist in this small area of space.
As his eyes drifted higher up the spire of the Arc Rift, he noticed that the bodies of the people became more crinkled, less blood showed, and their skin started to tear apart, leaving the shriveled remains of their internal organs to float away. Higher still, bodies had simply become skeletons. Some of them were dull gray, but others were a shiny white.
The transpod started vibrating and creaking as he drew nearer still to the rift. He could feel the energy pulsing through his bones, through the car, intertwining everything with a divine force.
Then it touched the edge of the rift. The whole structure let out an ear-piercing screech as metal bent against metal, crumbling in as all the windows shattered. Zan's leg was immediately trapped, pinching with excruciating pain as little fractures spread across the pressure point until eventually it snapped.
Another burst of pain from his eye, as one of the pieces of glass landed itself there, no bigger than a fingernail clipping. The transpod was beginning to crumple faster, and some of the pieces of metal were now pressing against his chest, and one particularly uncomfortable one was pressing against the back of his head.
It imploded. All the metal immediately snapped as the pressure became too great, twisting the vehicle in, in a way that made it look like a three-fingered claw. A thin stream of blood rose from it as it hovered just 2 inches above the ground.
Zan's consciousness fluttered for a moment as his head was simultaneously impaled with a piece of metal and crushed between two others. Then he woke up with no previous memory. He was young, probably still 2 to 3 years away from his teen years. There was an immense pain in his head. He pulled himself up, which was incredibly easy on the lunar surface. He had a flicker of déjà vu, but it was gone soon enough.
