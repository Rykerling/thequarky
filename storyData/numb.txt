Oni didn't feel anything as she slammed into the thorn bush. She had been hunting deer because today was a hunting day. Every hunting day, all the hunters went out into different parts of the forest with their bows and arrows to hunt something.
She was hiding behind a tree, waiting to hear the movement of some creature. Her bow was already loaded, and she was ready to turn around and shoot at a moment's notice. She always made sure she was shooting an animal first. Everyone did. They didn't want to accidentally shoot another one of their own kind.
She heard something walking through the crunchy snow. She turned around, and it was a deer. She aimed and released at almost lightning speed, but she didn't aim well enough. The arrow lodged itself in a tree behind the deer, scaring it off.
Oni started running after the deer. While she was running, she reloaded her bow and arrow and aimed again. The deer jumped off a small ledge, and she followed.
As it turned out, the ledge was a small waterfall on a river that fed into a small pond.
She slipped on the top of the ice and launched forward onto the flat surface. Instinct let her catch herself, but her momentum still propelled her forward over the slick ice. Her face slammed into the thorn bush.
The blood coming from her forehead started to drip down into her eyes, making it hard to see. She pulled herself out of the bush. She used her fur coat to wipe the blood from her eyes, but new blood quickly replaced it.
The bush was covered in small berries, so at least she wouldn't have to go home completely empty-handed. She grabbed as many berries as she could and stuffed them into her pocket. She then made the long trek back to her village.
Her village was a moderately sized group of huts built around trees. On the outside, they looked like flattened cones that rose to about twice the height of the tallest person in the village. Their diameter was almost thirty people long.
On the inside, the tallest parts were used for storing things like coats, shirts, and furs that could be used to make other things. Around the perimeter of the inside of the flattened cone, at about half a person's height, there was a wall. On the other side of this wall was plenty of storage space for food. This area was not as insulated, so it kept the food cold, preventing it from spoiling. The inside of this wall was lined with beds.
Usually, in the space between, where it was just a little taller than the average person, there was a stone fireplace for drying meat and preventing them from freezing to death.
Since none of Oni's people could feel anything, it was very important to pay attention to everything else happening to them. Usually it was safe inside the huts as long as they kept the door closed.
Still, if somebody was ill, they got to sleep near the fires, and it usually worked. They also had many remedies and basic medicines to help cure certain sicknesses that seemed common among their people.
She emptied her pockets into the berry storage cabinet across the hut from her bed. She then went over to Ki and asked if he wanted to play a game.
Despite being in the middle of nowhere, her people had it all figured out. They had plenty of time to do whatever they wanted. This mostly included many different forms of art and games.
He pulled out a beautifully decorated game board along with some intricately carved pieces. Ki always made his own boards and pieces. He liked making his own games in general. This game involved sliding pieces up and down and side to side on a fairly large grid.
This was an average day for Oni. For the next three days she did what she had been doing her whole life. She played games, carved her bow, ate food, and slept.
Then a stranger came into the village. She knew there were other villages, but she had never seen one or anybody from one.
"People!" he exclaimed. "I have some very bad news for you."
"What is it?" asked Ki.
"They are coming! They have destroyer mechanisms!"
"Who are coming?"
"The killers! They've come for us!"
Everyone in the village started freaking out. Oni had heard legends of the killers. She had heard that they came to villages and destroyed them with great fires. She had heard they set up mines to gather valuable resources. She had heard they were horrible, mindless creatures.
"You must leave your village," said the outsider.
Oni ran to her tent and grabbed her backpack. She filled it with all the dried meat and berries that she could. She grabbed her puffiest jacket. It wasn't because it was comfortable. It was because the extra padding kept in warmth, and she didn't know which direction they would be going.
The other villagers had done the same. As a final thing, she grabbed her bow along with a backup string, fifty-seven arrows, a special mechanism that could easily shave down sticks into arrows, and a small bag of feathers for attaching to those arrows.
She joined the group of people who were ready to leave the village. As soon as everyone had gathered, they left.
Soon afterward they heard rumbling sounds in the opposite direction from where they were going. Oni looked back and saw a giant floating machine. It had a large oval-shaped object with a small metal tube connected to the bottom. The metal tube had many clear windows.
Below it, a pillar of flame shot up into the sky in the distance. Another rumbling sound and another pillar of fire, this one closer, reminded them to keep moving. They kept moving through the forest and didn't dare stop to make camp until very late, when some of the members were beginning to fall asleep while standing. They all slept next to each other so they wouldn't freeze to death that night.
The next morning they got up and started crunching through the hard snow. They were determined to go onward with a will stronger than anything Oni had ever seen before.
There was smoke rising from a dangerously close location. The sky was a marvelous blue dotted with only a few white clouds.
However, just a few hours later, in the distance, like a wall of death, a storm was approaching.
"We need to find cover!" shouted one of the people in the group.
A murmur of agreement ran through the rest of the group. The area was becoming more hilly, but it had just as much forest.
"We could split up," said one of the other members of the group.
"Or we could just try building shelter here," said another.
"Whoever wants to find shelter can attempt to do so, but when the distance between the storm and us has halved, meet us back here. We will be working on shelter, but if you find something that you think would work, make sure to tell us," said Arn as he stepped up onto a rock so everyone could hear.
Arn was the leader of Oni's group. A few of the people split off from the group, but many more stayed behind to work on the shelters. Luckily, many people had brought axes, and the chopping sounds filled the evening.
A small hut was built around the largest tree in the clearing, and it was tied together with a long stretch of rope somebody had brought. Looking back, there were many things Oni could have brought that she didn't. Three small fire pits were built and then lit to warm the area.
The searchers returned, saying they had found only one small cave. They said it could fit about half the group along with a fire. Caves were not as protected from the elements, but they were stronger and held heat better. One could also keep the fire lit in a cave, whereas in the hut the fire would have to be put out as the last person went to sleep. Otherwise, the fire could go out of control and burn down the hut with everyone in it.
Oni decided to stay in the hut. The majority of the people also wanted to stay in the hut. Lots of body heat would be generated since her people produced many times the amount of body heat as normal people. This was why they could survive in colder environments with thinner fleeces. They also had much thicker skin and a layer of fat between their skin and the rest of their bodies. Despite this, they were mostly skinny. The outside of their skin usually became cold enough to get frostbite, and when they were very young, they would often become too cold in general and destroy their nerves.
This was why they couldn't feel anything. They had tried raising children to be able to feel, but it had never worked. The cold always got to them one time or another.
That night, as the storm started to pick up, Oni chewed on her chewy meat stick. Even though their skin couldn't feel anything, their mouths could still taste, so food was perfected when it was made.
"Are you okay?" asked Ki. "You've been chewing on that bit of meat for some time and haven't swallowed yet."
"I'm just scared," replied Oni, "and angry at the killers. I can't believe they just came in and destroyed our village so quickly. It still feels like a sleep vision, even though it's been happening for a few days now."
"Yeah, I understand," replied Ki.
"Really?" asked Oni. "Because it seems like you've been perfectly fine this whole time."
"Well, so have you," said Ki. "Want to hear a secret?"
"Why not?" she said.
"I wish I had feeling," said Ki. "I wish I could know what it felt like to be warm or cozy or know what something soft feels like."
"Oh," said Oni.
She had never thought of it that way. She always thought feeling meant pain. She always thought it would slow you down and make your life worse. Nobody could remember the time when they could feel anything, so all they had was speculation. They had stories of people who had been able to feel, but nobody had ever experienced it in real life. They described pain as something unbearable but also something you had to bear. It was like that feeling you got in your gut when you got into a fight with a friend, but hundreds of times worse. She never thought it could feel good. She never thought about how things would feel after the pain.
The wind started whooshing outside, and the structure started to creak.
"We should probably get to bed now," said Ki.
"Yeah," replied Oni. "We probably should."
The next day they all got up and went to the cave. Only a few of the people on the outside died from the cold. The rest were still alive. They continued onward, away from the constantly nearing explosions and floating machines.
The land was still very hilly, but the trees were becoming less dense. Soon they could see a large part of the ground missing in the distance. As they got closer, they realized it was a canyon with a large frozen river at the bottom. They knew how to get across. It would be very difficult, and surely some lives would be lost.
They climbed down the sheer rock. Oni didn't like doing this, but it was the only way to escape. An explosion nearby shook the rock, and some pebbles fell loose. One or two people fell off the cliff, but they were caught by the other climbers.
After what seemed like an eternity, they made it to the bottom. There was a small bank, but stretching across the wide valley was a large, flat sheet of ice. Beneath the ice was a cold, dark river.
A few of the people started tentatively walking on the ice. When it didn't break, more joined. The ice was very rough and covered in snow. The fluctuating temperatures over the last few days had made large craters and bumps as big as a hand, so she knew their boots would have good traction.
Like all the others, she tentatively started walking across the ice. They all knew the dangers that frozen ice over liquid water held, and they were all being very careful. The very first people who had stepped onto the ice were now getting off at the other end.
At that exact moment, another explosion shook the world. The boom was expected, but the sound afterward was unlike anything they had ever heard. It was kind of a cracking sound, but it was strange and distorted, though not muffled. In fact, it was a very sharp sound, even if it wasn't high-pitched. This was the sound of cracking ice.
The ice normally wouldn't have cracked, but with ninety-five people on it, it was already under a tremendous amount of pressure.
Oni felt the ice shift under her feet as more cracking sounds filled the air. Another boom in the distance set it all in motion. Now she could see the ice shifting all around. The ice downstream was also starting to break.
Most of the people had stopped moving, but some of them had bolted toward the shore. The ice was now starting to tilt downward, and a visible crack had formed. She could hear the water rushing underneath. She could see part of the ice rising on one end, and in the other direction she could see it slowly sinking under. This was because of all their weight. Even more cracking sounds filled the air as the ice strained with all its might not to fall apart.
The ice on their side had now gone down so far that it was starting to slide under the other ice. This was causing the other ice to crack. The sun beat down with all of its intensity, melting the ice and making it even weaker.
Slowly everyone who wasn't moving, including Oni, was being pushed toward the small foot-high wall of ice. The more their chunk of ice slid under, the more its slope increased.
There was now a large tilt to their chunk of ice. As the small ledge of ice was about to hit them, Oni and Ki stepped onto the higher ice. A large portion of the ice chunk was now floating in midair with water cascading down both sides. The people who were still on the ice chunk, which was now at almost a 20° angle, started slipping on the newly rushing water.
Some of them were swept off their feet, and despite trying to grab onto anything, they slid toward the small ledge. They could not feel pain, but they could definitely feel fear.
Another explosion happened nearby. This time the shockwave was amplified through the breaking ice, and it shook everyone with a jarring movement. The large tilted piece of ice broke in half. The back end, which was off the water, fell down with a giant splash. The water rushed over the ice on one side, and on the other it pushed against the still-tilted ice that was wedged underneath the main river ice.
Like a giant lever, it pushed up, and the ice around Oni ruptured into many pieces. The ice nearby and downstream also started breaking as new ice slammed into it. Some of the ice slid over other ice, while other pieces slammed into each other and pulverized themselves.
Small groups of people were on each of the ice chunks as they started to accelerate down the river. Everywhere they went, new chunks of ice slammed into the main chunks, breaking them down into even more pieces.
The hot sun was also melting much of the snow upstream, causing the stream to be even more rapid than usual. This also helped in the destruction of the ice.
One of the larger chunks of ice that held a large group of people slammed into the main ice sheet. Chunks immediately started breaking off it as the people on the original ice chunk slid forward.
One of them fell off and was sucked under the ice that would break to any force but his. The others all climbed onto new ice chunks. Fear wrapped around Oni's heart as she sped downstream.
In just 10 minutes, the heat had caused their iceberg to halve in size. The surface ice was also becoming much weaker. The icebergs were starting to slow down. Finally, after an entire hour, their iceberg hit the bank of the river, and they were able to get off.
They regrouped with the rest of their people who had been lost. It wasn't all of them. It wasn't even half of them. It was only 23 people, including Oni and Ki.
Together, they agreed to climb up the wall. From there, they might be able to see the others from the high vantage point. The sky was turning orange now, and only a small sliver of it could be seen through the top of the canyon. They started climbing the wall. One of them pulled himself over the edge.
A loud bang startled all of them. Oni's heart skipped a beat. She was not far behind. The guy who had pulled himself up now staggered backward with a shocked expression on his face and fell backward off the cliff.
Oni peeked over the edge. There was a large metal structure with a large hole in the ground next to it. It was in the distance, but there were little tents set up everywhere. The metal structure was half brass-colored and half iron-colored. There were large machines in the pit that seemed to be breaking the rock.
Above it was one of the large floating mechanisms. There were unfamiliar people wearing thick fur jackets walking about everywhere. They were the killers. One of them was facing toward the edge and pointing a small metal object at the edge of the cliff. Oni ducked down as something very small struck the edge of the cliff at incredible speed.
"Get to the river!" shouted somebody.
Immediately everyone started climbing down. The man holding the strange metal object advanced to the edge, and it started making loud noises. Some of the people around her started to go limp and simply fell off the cliff.
When Oni was close enough to the ground, she jumped down and bolted for the river. The river would carry them downstream at a fairly fast pace. She dove toward the bottom because she knew the stronger currents were down there. She swam forward as fast as she could for as long as she could, and then she had to come up to breathe.
When she reached the surface, she started banging on the ice. It cracked, but it was too slippery, and she kept moving downstream too fast. She banged even harder, but it still only almost cracked.
Then she had an idea. She grabbed her bow and pulled an arrow from the quiver. She pulled back and let go, and the arrow shot through the water, leaving behind a tiny trail of bubbles that quickly disappeared. It lodged itself in the ice, and she was able to grab onto it.
Once again she banged on the ice, but this time she was able to strike it again, and then a third time. The ice broke. She stuck her head through and gasped for air.
She looked down and saw Ki struggling in the current, trying to get up. Nobody in the group knew how to swim, but Oni had just kind of picked it up in the moment.
Oni dove down and grabbed his hand. She started pulling him back up, but it was hard. He was starting to go limp. She positioned him so he could easily hang onto her neck.
She pulled out another arrow and shot it into the ice. She struggled even harder to move upward as the current pulled her down and Ki away. Ki had almost let go. She thrust upward with all her might and was just able to grab the arrow as she was pulled by. With a final burst of energy, she broke through the ice. She pulled Ki through. She knew they would die of hypothermia very soon.
Oni pulled them into a small nook in the rock and lit a fire using an arrowhead and the long metal tool used for carving straight rods. There were a few trees on the bank, and she was able to easily find wood from them.
She hoped the cave was enough to prevent the killers from finding them by the fire. The trees on the banks would also hide them from other directions.
Oni huddled close to him. She looked up at the stars. They seemed to twinkle back down at her. The fire crackled.
He is going to survive, she told herself.
They were going to survive.
