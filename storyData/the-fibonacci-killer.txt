The virus rushed in, almost knocking me over. It quickly pressed from all sides, and I could feel it entering my systems. I let go of the restraints I had put on myself to prevent the fracturing.

Every square inch of air within my close proximity broke. It released a sound like low lightning that seemed to echo in higher pitches as some of the areas broke even more. The fractures seemed to push themselves further out. I tried to focus the fracturing energy outwards in the direction of the virus, and once again, my third attempt was best.

The larger fractures traveled through much better than the smaller ones, shattering the virus in two waves. There was a satisfying sense of fulfillment in me as I watched it break into pieces no bigger than a millimeter, and then drift apart in a cloud of small particles. I had killed the Fibonacci killer.

From there, things went very smoothly. I was highly respected for killing the Fibonacci killer, and I was able to get leadership of the town very fast. Esti still doesn’t know that he was the creator of the virus, and I don’t think I’ll tell him ever. He would probably be very upset, just as I was.

I shut off heaven fairly quickly, which a lot of people were mad about, but I had thought of a better way to live life. We would rebuild Eden. I had discovered that the computers under the city were much more powerful than I had first assumed, mostly because I did not know how the scale translated to computational power.

If I used the computers along with my program, I could rebuild Eden in a matter of weeks, and then, as soon as the war ended, we could go back to living normal lives. I could use the power to remove the particles from us, making us truly human again.

I planned to always stay watch over Eden, but I guessed many people would want to go off into the world. I would turn off the wall that led to the rocky landscape outside, letting whatever would come, be able to enter and exit the city.

With me in charge, Eden would become safe. Maybe Eden would expand, becoming a much larger city. I would try and fertilize the soil so that plant life could flourish between the buildings, and maybe I would be able to clear the atmosphere above it, allowing it to be one of the few places in the world that has sunshine.

Right now, however, I needed to work on the little things. I had to fix the computers, add good entrances, and exits to the sub-city computer level. I could probably get many people to work on the computers, as they were just as knowledgeable as me. It would give them something to do without heaven.

We carried charging vines with us, something I probably should have thought of when I ran away. The fog glowed brighter around our charging vines, which was very nice for fixing the computers.

Collectively, with a great amount of effort, we placed the computer boxes back where they had come from. Most of the metal parts lined up, and we hoped it would be enough to get the computers running.

It was. I hooked myself up to the computer and let my power flow through all of it. I could immediately feel the enormous scale with my power. I started to use it on a small scale, just to get a hang of stuff, but then I started doing bigger scale manipulations. I started putting the mist particles together. I started rebuilding Eden.
