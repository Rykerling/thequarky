Aira lived in City 62. City 62 was just a really large group of tall, pillar-like buildings. Surrounding City 62 was a vast expanse of sand.
There was a small oasis a mile or so away from the city, but it didn't really serve any purpose. A few date trees grew around the oasis. There were also many other trees, but the date trees were the only ones that were worth anything.
Food was scarce in the city. This was because they couldn't produce any food of their own, so they had to rely on food shipments. Unfortunately, the airships used lots of fuel to get from the jungle all the way to City 62.
The Superiors, as they called themselves, had extracted all the oil, coal, and other fuels that were essential in making everything work. Without fuel, there was no electricity, and without electricity, none of their machines or protection systems worked.
This wasn't as big of a concern for City 62 because they had no creatures that would try to attack the city. Unfortunately, it meant that the food station near the jungle, which did have creatures that would attack, needed to divert all of its energy to weapons.
Aira had heard they were cutting down the jungle and using the wood as fuel. This was really smart, except it still didn't produce enough fuel to send cargo airships all the way into the middle of the desert, and they were destroying the place where that food came from.
She had also heard they had figured out a new way to create energy. Large sheets of the newest technology, known as solar panels, were able to convert sunlight into energy. This would be much more efficient than burning fuel, and they were much lighter. Along with the fact that they could be added to almost anything and easily mass-produced, they seemed like the perfect solution.
They were mining most of the resources for the solar panels up in the Arctic. She didn't like to worry about all the things that were happening across the world, though. She just hoped those things would make it to them before the hunger did.
She was always hungry. The daily rations were only handed out once a day, and she had already eaten hers.
Below the city, the big machinery clunked away. At the base of each tower was a mechanism that pumped water through big tubes inside the towers. The water would absorb all the heat before being siphoned back down. That way, the temperatures inside the buildings were still at least somewhat bearable.
Unfortunately, some of these mechanisms were breaking down, and it was getting increasingly harder to fix them. The people who could repair them were demanding important resources such as extra rations and water.
The Power Column had the biggest and loudest pump. This was because it was the tallest tower in the entire city. All the important people lived in that tower. They were mostly just leaders, but the mechanics who worked on the giant pumps usually got a spot there too.
As for Aira, she lived in Second Class. People were rated by how tall the building their apartment was in happened to be. There were six different classes, plus the leader group that lived in the Power Column. The next richest or most powerful people lived in Class One. These were the tallest towers, not including the Power Column.
Class Two towers were half the size of Class One. After that, Class Three towers were half the size of Class Two. The Class Six towers were only a few stories high. They were built quickly without many materials or the help of good architects. They didn't have any pumps, so many people simply blew the walls off their houses and used them as shade instead.
As resources declined, power became less and less based on the height of your tower and more on whether you had those resources and were willing to give them away. People were pretty sure Johnson, the leader of the city, had an entire stockpile of food at the very top of his tower. He didn't really do much anymore because he was so quickly losing power.
Aira had a lot of time on her hands since nothing really happened in the city. Although, it might have just seemed like that because she had been living this life for so long.
Then she noticed a couple of people at the railing pointing out into the desert. Walking over, she looked out as well. About a mile away, emerging from a hole near the oasis, was an enormous mob of dark red creatures. She couldn't make out many details, but they were fast. They were quickly crossing the desert.
They were headed straight for City 62.
She could faintly hear an incredibly high-pitched screech that was barely audible, yet still hurt her ears as the creatures reached the Level One houses.
People were running, screaming, or hiding in small crevices everywhere. Aira sprinted to the nearest house, opened the door, and shoved herself into an empty food cabinet.
She could hear the screams from outside.
She stepped out into the city with many other people and surveyed the damage. It was barely noticeable. The dark red creatures seemed to have only gone up to the Level One buildings, taken about half the people there, and dragged them back into the desert.
Long streaks of blood were visible, even from the tops of the towers. They all led to one place.
The hole near the oasis.
Soon, the sound of people talking and yelling was louder than ever. No matter which conversation she listened to, there seemed to be only one message; What if they were next?
Down in the lower levels, many people were still hiding. Aira had always been someone who wanted facts. She wasn't very smart, and she didn't care about learning random, useless things, but when something was important, facts were the only thing she needed.
She decided to talk to the people who had gotten a more firsthand experience: the Class Six dwellers. Even though the volume of conversation was far above normal, the streets and bridges were somewhat empty.
Many of the people who had gone into hiding were still there and would be for a while. She walked down the spiral staircase that led to the base of the buildings. At the bottom, she could feel the stairs vibrating and hear the little bolts jiggling in their holes, trying to support the enormous weight of the tower.
She was almost halfway to the Class Six towers when a thought suddenly crossed her mind; What if they come back right now?
Once the thought entered her head, she had trouble getting it out. She tried telling herself it was completely illogical. Everyone else would be having the same thoughts and trying to reach the higher, safer towers. But then her stupid mind suggested they might simply be animals hunting, and that they could come back at any time for more food.
Her pace toward the outer buildings slowed, which she knew was stupid because it meant she would be out in the open even longer.
One thing still didn't make sense to her.
Why had they only come out now?
She had never heard of these creatures before, so how had they just appeared out of nowhere?
She puzzled over the question as she reached the Class Six towers. All she had to do was walk into a house and open a few cabinets until she found a tall, middle-aged man stuffed uncomfortably into a food cabinet.
"What happened?" she asked in what she tried to make sound like an authoritative voice, though it came out mostly shaky.
The man was still shivering with fear, and his response came out in stammers.
"Th-those m-monsters j-just came and k-killed him," he said, staring at a puddle of blood on the floor.
"Who was him?" asked Aira.
"I-I d-didn't know h-him. H-he j-just ran i-into my house l-looking for a hiding s-spot, b-but didn't m-make it."
"What did they look like?" she pressed.
"I-it was like they d-didn't have s-skin. I-it went s-straight to the m-meat, w-which had b-been cut up and c-crusted over l-like a scab! Th-they d-didn't have a-any eyes! They are d-demons, I t-tell ya... d-demons!"
She had heard enough.
"I'm gonna just go back to the city now," she said.
Her voice was shaking even more now. Turning away, she started running as the man kept yelling, "They're demons, I tell ya! Demons!"
She decided she was going to eavesdrop on the important people's conversation. She always liked listening in on those meetings, although now the matters were much more serious.
Climbing into her special hiding spot once she made it back into the tall towers, she could hear people yelling through the thin sheet of metal separating her from the conference room.
For some reason, there was a small hole in the middle that she could always peek through, but it never really helped since its view wasn't of the table. Instead, it faced a small metal drawer filled with who knew what.
"We'll build a wall around our city!" one person shouted.
"That won't work! We don't have enough time or resources!" another replied.
"What if we tinker with our small heat-seeking missiles so they can go into the caves and blow them up?" suggested a third.
"We can do that?"
"We have missiles?"
"Yeah. From the time we settled here. We thought there were gonna be creatures, so we got missiles to protect ourselves."
"Will they still work?"
"They should."
"Well then, let's get tinkering!"
With that, the meeting was concluded.
She had made it just in time for the end.
The mechanics who had attended rushed off toward some secret room buried deep beneath the city, or somewhere equally mysterious.
Aira paused to think about what was happening.
They were going to launch missiles into the cave systems and hope they blew some of the creatures up.
Humans were incredibly stupid when they were scared.
First, they would never have enough missiles to kill every single one of them, even if the blasts could collapse entire caverns. Second, the survivors would simply become even angrier and attack the city harder.
Unfortunately, there was nothing she could do now.
As she walked down the hallway, she heard someone talking on the opposite side of a door. Like many others, this person was asking for backup from the nearest grassland station.
Rumor had spread that only one ship had been sent to evacuate the city. It was a small ship, and it wouldn't carry many people, so already crowds were simply waiting for it to arrive.
Later that day, the missiles were ready. First, she felt a quivering all around her. It felt like being close to the generators, but she was nowhere near them. She was at the top of a tower that had vibration-dampening springs throughout the walls.
Then she saw them. Like new buildings slowly rising up from around the other towers, twelve pointed missiles, spewing fire out the back, rose into the sky. They slowly gained speed. Once they were high enough, they turned and headed straight for the hole in the ground. Every single one of them seamlessly entered it and disappeared into the abyss.
Aira watched the spot where they had disappeared, completely unaware that one of the missiles was making its way straight back toward the city, where one of the largest colonies of the scabbed blind creatures lived.
Less than a minute later, the missile headed beneath the city detonated. At first, it only shook the city. Sounds of metal bending could immediately be heard. Then cracking sounds came up from the ground. Next, the power column started to tilt with the ear-piercing sound of metal bending against its will. Aira's tower also started tilting. In fact, it seemed like all the towers were tilting slightly toward the center of the city.
She ran as fast as she could. As she sprinted across the metal bridge, it started to bend and twist. By the time she reached the next building, the staircase was also beginning to bend and crease. She sprinted down the metal staircase, holding the railing as the building it was attached to tilted even more precariously.
Looking around, she could see large cracks forming in the rock, and the whole ground seemed to be caving in. She made it to the ground and ran away from the center of the city. Large pieces of metal from the towers were beginning to fall.
More and more people fled from the center of the city. Soon, an entire crowd was pushing its way outward. Then the power column started to fall. It toppled directly toward Aira.
"Look out!" she yelled.
Everybody glanced up for the briefest moment. The power column was picking up speed fast.
Just then, a giant tremor shook the ground, knocking everybody down. They quickly got back up and changed course so they wouldn't be crushed by the power column.
The power column slammed into the tops of the surrounding towers. Large chunks of metal were thrown from both the power column and the towers. Everywhere, it seemed as though the power column had simply fallen apart.
Instead of one massive section crashing to the ground, many enormous pieces made up of multiple stories of the building slammed into the earth. They crumpled like tin cans under a foot on impact. The glass from the windows shot outward.
Aira dove behind another building just as the storm of glass rushed past. A few pieces grazed her skin, and three more lodged themselves in her leg.
She ran again. The pain was drowned out by the adrenaline running through her veins.
Behind her, the towers leaned so far inward that their tops crashed into one another.
Finally, she and a large crowd reached the outer edge of the city, where the ground was no longer crumbling. From there, they could finally look back.
The center of the city had already completely collapsed, and the surrounding towers were crumbling and falling inward. A wide trail of destruction marked where the power column had fallen.
The sun was beginning to set, reflecting off the dust and the copper plating around the silver buildings.
Then, off in the distance, a silver speck grew larger. As it got closer, she could tell it looked like an elongated sphere, almost pointed at both ends. On the bottom was a smaller tube lined with windows. It was a blimp airship.
Rescue had finally come.
Then dark red creatures came swarming out of the city. There were almost ten times as many as in the first raid. They were angry.
Immediately, the horde of people she was trapped in bolted toward the airship. She ran with them.
The airship was quickly approaching, and it must have spotted them because it began to descend. Then people realized they couldn't even fit a third of the group inside.
The people at the front kept running, but those toward the back were falling farther and farther behind.
Aira was near the front, but not all the way. She would be an easy target for anyone to take out.
The airship landed not far away. The first people climbed aboard. Aira was not far behind. She turned back as more people climbed onto the ship. It filled quickly, but there were still hundreds of people running toward it.
People were punching each other, while others were being trampled. The ship started to lift off as the horde of people below became a bloodbath.
Off to one side, avoiding the bloodshed, was Nile. He was one of the few people she knew. The look of hopelessness in his eyes as the airship lifted off stung her to the core.
But there was nothing she could do.
The captain announced that they were heading toward the jungle instead of back toward the plains, since there was a shortage of workers there.
Aira watched her home, her friends, and the only life she'd ever known disappear into the sunset.
