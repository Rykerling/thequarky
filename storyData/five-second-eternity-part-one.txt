“So how does this technology work?” asked the news reporter on the TV.
“A new leap in quantum computing: a particle that doesn’t really react with reality, but can affect certain parts of computers, and can replace electrons. There’s another cool aspect about this particle. It is unbound by time,” replied the other guy on TV.
“What does this mean? Like, explain it to the average mind.”
“Basically, on a computer, a bunch of electrons go around in a circuit. Even though these electrons go super fast, it still takes time for them to get from point A to point B. Computers have gotten extremely fast since 2038, so now their only limitation is the speed of these electrons, a big leap in digital computing, and a huge development for the gaming industry.”
“And what does this mean for your company, Mindsquare?”
“Brains also use electrons to think and act, just like computers. The electrons in the mind can be replaced with these instant particles, thus powering the speed of thought up to an infinite amount.”
“So you’re going to make people with infinite thought speed?”
“No. The body could not take that, and it would feel as if you’re frozen in time. No, no, no, instead we send people into a programmable dream state, with the accuracy of being fully conscious and even more. A person could live 5 billion years in just five seconds. In fact, they could live even more. They could live an infinite amount of time. They could become a God of their own universe, they could build their world and live in it, in a state of pure bliss, for all eternity.”
“What would happen when they reintegrate into reality? It seems like an experience like that would mess them up pretty bad.”
“About that… they die. The overstimulation of the mind in five seconds basically fries it. Once you begin, there’s no turning back.”
“And what if something goes wrong? Can you stop it, or will they be forced to live in, uh, hell? Forever?”
“We take great precaution to make sure that doesn’t happen. That is how we can guarantee it won’t.”
Jyson turned off his EyePod. He had become increasingly disappointed in society, and humanity as a whole. The world was descending into chaos farther than humanity had ever gone before. Five wars in the last 10 years, three of them reaching the severity of World War II. The only thing making up for it was the fact that building technology was now so advanced that devastated cities could turn into new beautiful forests of architecture in just a matter of weeks. And now that people had gotten into a peaceful place, they just wanted to die.
To him, and the rest of the living world, everybody who had decided to chip was already dead. If someone truly thought about it, it was very mind-boggling and hard to wrap their heads around, but the concepts were getting easier, as they were being taught more widely. Schools now talked about it, and it was everywhere on the news as a glorious solution to the computer decay problem of the singularity.
That was a problem engineers had been trying to fix for a long time as the singularity grew nearer. Basically, as humans became more dependent, or even just more machine than human, other machines would have to take over businesses, construction, and the maintaining of the rest of the world, while humans slowly became lazier or more hooked up to machines. The problem was that eventually the technology would decay, leaving humanity helpless. The singularity meant we were sure to die. But the option to chip had become an amazing solution, especially since they were figuring out how to upload multiple people into the same universe.
Jyson didn’t understand the point of it. In his mind, it was just some time dilation trick, similar to the effects of what would happen on drugs. In fact, people most likely lived their entire lives, all the infinite years of them, in a state similar to dreaming instead of being fully conscious. Those people could just fry their brains immediately and say that they were living in eternity; there was no way to actually know unless you decided to chip.
Meanwhile, the real world had become a wasteland in many parts for those who had not decided to chip. Technology was quickly deteriorating, just like the predictions, and pollution wasn’t getting any better either. Jyson’s little part of the world was a forest made of the burnt remains of pine trees. It was very cold, but fires ignited everywhere they could. It didn’t rain anymore, and lightning was generated by large amounts of dust and ash in the atmosphere instead of little water droplets.
Social media had become a big part of society too. With all the rich people going to chip, and the poor people unable to do such a thing, they turned to the next best thing: social media. Most of the time it was pretty horrible. Everybody always seemed to be in some sort of fight. However, it did have its upsides. It allowed him to see that large parts of the world had turned to a different solution. That’s where he was headed. Large cities had been built and filled with people who still believed in mortality. People like him.
That night he found a lightning rod and charged his EyePod, a little dome that went over one eye, projecting some of the endless stream of social media into his mind. He used his ax to cut down a tree, eating the bitter wood that hadn’t quite burned yet. He went to sleep, and was able to get a full 10 hours. Danger was nonexistent in this world, other than that of starvation and dehydration. He woke up and walked for the next 14 hours.
The social media had changed its topics, as the people who were talking about chipping ran out of stuff to say or ended up doing the procedure. That just left disputes of whatever else people had left to argue about. It was becoming increasingly stressful, but he had nothing else to do but walk and scroll endlessly on his EyePod.
A week later he made it. The city was comprised of massive towers anywhere from 100 to 300 stories tall. Many of them were glowing, even though it was day, meaning they had some sort of sustainable energy source to pull from. There was a big wall covered in spikes around it, along with gates, which were currently closed.
Jyson walked up to the gates and found a speaker on them. “Hello?”
He waited a while before the gates opened, and a mechanical voice replied, “Please walk through our metal detector. Leave all metal items on a tray to be scanned.”
He did as he was told, and when the machine found no weapons other than his ax, which it said he could retrieve if he ever left the city, but for now it would be kept in a safe vault, he grabbed his EyePod and wandered into the city.
It was beautiful and overwhelming. No sunlight made it down that far, but it was still intensely bright because of all the building lights. There were gardens with actual food everywhere. The air smelled kind of metallic and electric, but he also smelled lots of spices and cooking everywhere. Everybody seemed to be doing something. Everybody had a role in the city.
The buildings got taller as he went deeper into the city, but the lights also got brighter, the smells stronger, and the gardens denser. He reached out and picked a squishy, pinkish-orange fruit from a tree in one of the large gardens. When he bit it, some sticky juice came out, but the taste was the most magnificent thing. It was so soft and sweet, so different from the insides of trees that he’d been living on for the last two years of his life.
A week later, he had figured out how to get someone to show him all around the city, telling him where everything was and how to get a job. He learned that the energy for the city came from the lightning storms and solar panels on the tops of buildings, along with geothermal heat from below. They were able to pump water from underground and filter it, and do so in quantity, but they also had systems for recycling water just in case. Finally, the guide told him about the city core. That’s where all the technological systems were. He said that’s where the chipping station was.
Jyson was surprised that they had a chipping station because the city was for the survivors, the people who were so against that technology. But if people were going to die eventually, it seemed like a merciful way to let them go. The guide, who saw his facial expressions, explained that despite what it looked like, the city was struggling in some ways, and viruses seemed to come in waves. During certain seasons, the food was scarce, so it was worth sacrificing a few for the greater good, and they only sacrificed those who volunteered anyway.
The chipping station still creeped him out, but for the next 13 months of his life, it wouldn’t play much of a role. Because he was poor, he got one of the higher rooms in the towers, but life was still pretty good.
Soon, though, there would be a revelation concerning the chipping process, and it would become much more relevant to his life.
