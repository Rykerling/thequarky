There are three kinds of people in this world. There are those who live in the past: every mistake they made, every alternative solution, everything that should have happened. Then there are the people who live in the future, always thinking about what they could do, all the potential that everything has, but never actually using it. And then, last, there are those who live in the present, but the number of people who do is small enough that oftentimes it’s not even considered a category.
The world is a very harsh place, and it often forces you to think in terms of the past and future, not the present. Maybe one day you will know what it is like to be addicted to the past and what it is like to be addicted to the future. I am addicted to the future, to thinking about what could be. This addiction is killing me, but I would like you to understand why. This is my story.
◇─◇──◇──◇─◇
It is cold. It always is in these regions, but today is especially cold. I am only 13 years old, so I doubt it is actually as cold as I thought it was, but it was certainly cold. The snowflakes drift down. I remember I used to like watching the snowflakes land on the metal grates and melt, just as the first snows of winter were falling.
Today, though, I was more concerned about my hunger. Most of the time people would give me food when they saw me and pitied me, but sometimes I was not so lucky and I had to steal, and even fewer times I was unlucky enough that I just wouldn’t eat for the day.
That was when I came across another kid. He was 17 at the time, and he looked almost like an adult to me. He was sitting next to a dumpster, shivering in a torn-up jacket. I approached him, and he looked up only when I got within his personal space.
“Do you have any food?” I remember asking him this incredibly well. I don’t know why, but I just do.
“Ha, what makes you think I would have food, and if I did, what makes you think I would share it with you?”
“Well, I don’t know. You’re just the only one I could find right now. Everyone else has gone to bed.”
“You should too. I don’t need another thing to deal with right now.”
◇─◇──◇──◇─◇
It had been a month. I think we had become friends. I know he liked me even though he did not show it. He was always making grumpy remarks, and I learned a lot of new bad words from him. I liked him too. He made me feel safer, he taught me how to break into restaurants and bakeries, and I ate best on the days I was with him. The next important moment I give you is right after we had plundered a bakery.
“I love the ones with the cherry filling the best,” I said.
“Meh, they are so tart.”
“That’s why I like them.”
“To each his own, I guess.”
“Yeah, I guess so.”
“So I suppose we’ve been wandering around together for long enough to ask you: where are you from?”
I stopped chewing for a moment to think. “I don’t really know where I came from. I was in an orphanage for a while, but it was pretty easy to run away. I didn’t like being trapped.”
“Do you regret it, running away?”
“No. I miss a lot of what was there, but I’ve traveled a long way, and the past isn’t real anymore. There’s no point in regretting it.”
“That’s a lot of wisdom for a young kid.”
“What about you?”
“Oh, well, I lived in an orphanage as well, although the one I’m from is more up north. It’s even colder there. I did something that I regret, and I ran away.”
“What did you do?”
“Well, I was at the top of my class for a while, and I had a friend. And then we got into a big fight, and... and...”
◇─◇──◇──◇─◇
He couldn’t finish, not for a long time. I didn’t bother him about it. Part of me didn’t want to know. It wasn’t until I found a bag of black powder that he brought it up again, about three months later.
“Hey, look what I found in the dumpster. Do you know what it is?” I went over to him, holding the bag with a little bit of black powder in the corner of it.
He grabbed the bag and threw it out into the wind, where it was carried away. “Never touch that. Never let it get into your body.”
“Why? What does it do?”
“It’s a drug called Kronospsychis. It makes you relive your past. Do you remember when I was telling you about how I got in a fight with one of my friends? And then I did something I regretted?”
“Yes?”
“I think it’s time you learn the whole story. It was around this time I had heard of Kronospsychis. I wasn’t completely aware of what it was, just that if I took it, I could go back and change my memory of what had happened.”
“I thought you said it was bad.”
“Be patient, I’m getting there. That night, I took it, and the argument replayed in my mind. I tried to make it go a better way, but negative emotions are much stronger than positive ones. By the time I had finished, the memory had only gotten worse. The argument had become much more dramatic than it actually had been. I tried to go back again, to fix it, to at least undo what I had done, but every time I only made it worse. A week later, it had grown out of proportion. I couldn’t sleep because I could only think about the argument. The bad memory had become a drug in and of itself.”
“How do you get out of a loop like that?”
“Well, it gets worse before it gets better. One day, my friend came to apologize. It was the first time we’d seen each other in a while; we lived on opposite sides of the orphanage. At this point, I hated him so much. I thought he had ruined my life. I thought he had done it intentionally. I don’t think you can understand my hate. So I punched him. I did it again, and again, and I didn’t stop for three minutes. And when I thought he was dead, I only felt a little bit of sadness.”
“It does get better, though, right?”
“Well, he made it to the hospital. He wasn’t actually dead, but he was pretty badly injured. I ran. I ran as far and as fast as I could. I made it to new, unfamiliar territory, and that made it easier to let go. I was able to stop taking Kronospsychis, to live in the present.”
◇─◇──◇──◇─◇
It was another year before he got hit by a car. He broke both of his legs and cracked a rib. The ambulance was there within an hour. I don’t know what happened to him once he got wheeled off to the hospital. Physically, he would be fine, I knew that. It was where he would end up that I was uncertain of.
Once he was in the system, they would probably get his age. He was now old enough to have his own house, but poor enough that it would have to be one of the cheap orphanage-funded houses down south, a few states over. That’s where most of the people like him went.
I wouldn’t be able to make it down there. It was much too far for a journey. I wouldn’t be able to make it onto public transportation that could get me down there, and it would take years to get there by foot, and even then I would not be able to find him.
So instead, I found Kronospsychis. I remembered his warnings, but he wasn’t here to stop me, and I wasn’t going into the past anyway. Instead, I built myself a future. One where somehow I made it down there, and I found him.
I’ve lived two years in this illusion, but it feels like so much longer. I have lived the best life I ever could have, building a future in a home, living with him. Living in the lie made me happier than I ever could have been in real life, and I got to live with him for a long time.
But over time it took more Kronospsychis to keep the illusion stable. I had to take more with each dose, until eventually the dose I had to take to keep the illusion stable was also enough to kill me. But I am okay with that. Even if it was an illusion, I have lived my best life.
